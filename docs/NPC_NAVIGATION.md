# Native NPC portal navigation

This is a local addition to the RC3 preview, not an engine-validated release. The original F10 awareness adapter only supplied attention and aiming. Navigation now provides a bounded approach, transfer and pursuit adapter for native ground NPCs.

## Behavior

Native NPCs with ground movement can consider a portal shortcut to their current enemy or existing entity navigation goal. Hostile players visible through a portal can also be discovered through the existing attention adapter. Friendly disposition is preserved. A shorter native route remains eligible, and an unreachable target can motivate a portal detour. Every proposal must bring the exit closer to the target; a long native path alone cannot send the NPC back and forth through the pair.

The engine builds and follows the path to the front of the entry. Starting behind the entrance does not exclude approaching its front. The native map node graph still determines whether that approach can be reached; the addon does not create map nodes or a NextBot navmesh. Facepunch documents this dependency for [NPC:SetLastPosition](https://wiki.facepunch.com/gmod/NPC:SetLastPosition) and [native schedules](https://wiki.facepunch.com/gmod/NPC:SetSchedule).

Wider portals supply additional crossing points across their horizontal opening, up to nine per link. The point count uses the usable width at both endpoints and the NPC's unchanged collision hull. Narrow links retain a central point. Points occupy the centers of lateral strips, leaving clearance for oblique crossings near the edges. Candidates are ranked by the combined approach and exit distance; a blocked center can leave a usable side crossing. Each point receives the same floor, aperture and obstruction checks. Resizing invalidates an existing approach, and the next routing attempt computes points from the new dimensions. This expands the ground crossing choices, not the native map node graph. `SP.NPCPortalRoutePoints(npc, entry, exit)` exposes the candidate plane points before floor and collision checks.

At the entrance, the complete movement hull must fit both apertures. The source sweep, exit corridor, destination hull and floor support are checked before moving the existing NPC. A small clearance above the floor accommodates the difference between native walking height and the entrance probe; the rise is swept for obstructions. The NPC retains its identity, health, relationships and weapon, stays upright, and resumes its enemy or entity goal at the exit. A successful transfer emits the existing `SeamlessPortalsTeleported` notification with kind `npc`, plus the endpoint outputs.

The final approach direction is measured from native position changes. The exit continues that direction after transforming it through the portal, traveling only far enough for the hull to clear the exit plane. Route planning checks the estimated incoming direction, and transfer rechecks the actual trajectory against both apertures and exit obstructions. A blocked diagonal is refused instead of substituting a perpendicular shove.

Clients draw complementary clipped portions of the NPC through the entrance and exit. The crossing takes the distance divided by the NPC's ideal movement speed. Native pursuit briefly waits at the destination during that interval, preventing exit movement from adding a burst of speed to the rendered crossing. The old path's stopping segment is replaced at the exit and [NPC:SetMoveDelay](https://wiki.facepunch.com/gmod/NPC:SetMoveDelay) pauses the motor. The copied approach animation advances with crossing distance; the engine selects the actual animation when pursuit resumes. The adapter does not force an intermediate sequence or cycle before that selection. The native waiting schedule, owned movement delay and playback rate are restored on completion, cancellation and reload, while replacement schedules and delays retain control.

The visual passage uses temporary client models with the NPC's model, scale, skin, bodygroups, materials and pose parameters, plus a bone-merged weapon copy where applicable. Inverse kinematics is disabled on these copies before changing their model; the real NPC's foot contacts are never reused across the two rooms. Drawing does not change the real NPC's position, sequence or cycle. After crossing, the copied skeleton blends into the native resumed pose over 0.25 seconds, allowing the engine to select another walking sequence without a sudden visual restart. Bones blend relative to their parent joints, preserving limb lengths across different gait poses. Clipping state is restored after drawing, including draw failures. Owned models are removed on completion or cancellation, and previous render overrides are restored only while the adapter still owns its wrapper.

This is a visual bridge over a complete movement-hull transfer: damage and collisions use the actual NPC at the destination during the short crossing, not two partial physical bodies. Visual passages are bounded to 32 NPCs and two seconds of crossing plus the final 0.25-second animation blend; slower crossings fall back to the immediate native handoff.

Each routing attempt considers at most four links and performs geometry checks for at most twelve candidate points. The adapter keeps at most 32 concurrent approaches and passages, the existing rotating NPC registry, a progress timeout and a retry delay. Attention proxies yield while the NPC approaches. Geometry changes, endpoint opt-outs, target invalidation, AI switches, another schedule taking control, reload and map cleanup retire pending routes.

## Settings and limits

- `seamless_portals_npc_navigation`: server, archived, default `1`.
- `seamless_portals_npc_distance`: existing route/sight distance limit, default `2048`.
- Both portal endpoints must enable `props`; native AI disable/ignore-player settings and player notarget flags are respected.
- Links must be approximately vertical and preserve world up. Floor/ceiling transport, flying NPCs, scripted NPCs and NextBots are not supported by this adapter.
- Each plan uses one portal link. Longer journeys may acquire another link after native navigation resumes; this is not global optimization over a portal graph.
- A route proposal does not prove that the engine can reach the entry. Missing nodes, unsuitable terrain or obstacles can cause the native approach to fail and fall back.

## Validation

The dedicated Sandbox probes on `gm_flatgrass` used real `npc_zombie` and `npc_zombie_torso` entities and a native bullseye target. They recorded approach movement, one completed transfer per successful case, and subsequent movement toward the original enemy. A torso also passed with a 100-unit portal, a 0.3-unit raised lip and a 0.1-degree tilt, reproducing the small floor-clearance difference found during interactive testing. The final motor-pause probe recorded a stable destination throughout the crossing, followed by normal pursuit. These probes have no human multiplayer observer.

A later dedicated probe increased a rectangular link's horizontal width from 100 to 256, 512 and 1000 units, recording 1, 3, 7 and 9 candidate points for a zombie, then one point after shrinking back to 100. Separate left, right and blocked-center cases each completed exactly one native traversal through a side point. The user subsequently confirmed the wider portal behavior, then requested preserving the incoming direction during the exit.

The separate offline suite, `validation/npc_navigation_tests.py`, covers route choice, torso hull clearance, approach from behind, duplicate transfer prevention, exit/source blockage, aperture/orientation rejection, endpoint changes, AI switches, hostility, script exclusion, schedule ownership, timeout, native entity goals and reload/cleanup. Its 37 cases also check complementary clipping, render-state restoration after errors, animation progression, delayed pursuit without forcing an intermediate animation, cancellation of the temporary wait, IK-free visual copies, final skeleton blending, limb length preservation and partial allocation cleanup. Width-dependent point counts, side crossings, blocked centers, smaller exits, hull-dependent density, resizing and bounded polygon checks have separate coverage. Further cases verify measured diagonal movement and rejection of a blocked or out-of-aperture diagonal exit. The full release validator includes it. API doubles do not establish native AI behavior.

In the user's singleplayer Sandbox `gm_construct` session, iterative manual testing covered the torso and full zombie, including corrections for sliding, excessive speed, position jumps, IK leg stretching and the final animation restart. The user accepted the animation quality for most tested NPCs and confirmed that removing the intermediate server animation change eliminated the renewed exit jump. An antlion exception motivated the subsequent joint-relative blend. A native client observer recorded 122 full-zombie blend samples and 37 torso samples with the real NPC's sequence and cycle unchanged by drawing. In the final recorded zombie frame, the copied foot was within 0.05 units of its native position at 99% blend. A later native antlion run recorded 42 draw samples; joint lengths during the final blend differed from the native skeleton by at most 0.031%. These observations support the local animation changes, not general animation compatibility.

A subsequent dedicated probe tested diagonal approaches from both sides, wide side lanes, a blocked center and a straight torso approach. All six completed exactly one traversal. The measured exit displacement matched the transformed incoming movement in each case (direction dot product within 0.000001 of one). The additional point counts remained 1, 3, 7 and 9 as width increased. The user confirmed the final direction change in the singleplayer session and approved the navigation revision for commit.

Native smoke ran in the listen server, its client and the existing dedicated server. These checks used hotload; clean startup and automatic client file delivery have not been validated. Additional NPC classes, map node layouts and multiplayer observers remain acceptance work.
